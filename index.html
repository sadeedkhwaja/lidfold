Six effects

Pick how your desktop leaves

Switch any time from the menu bar. Each one follows the physical angle of your lid, so it's never a canned animation.

Duo

The iPhone Duo look. Your desktop swells from the hinge, softens and fades into black.

Ghost

The screen tilts over the hinge while your desktop stays fixed in space behind it.

Roll

Pixels curl up from the top and roll down toward the hinge.

Shutter

Six rigid panels slide down and stack behind each other.

Flex

The display bows inward like a sheet being pressed in the middle.

Iris

Eight blades close over the desktop like a camera aperture.

Install

Three steps, takes a minute

LidFold isn't signed with a paid Apple developer account, so macOS shows one extra warning the first time. It's normal, and it's the same for every free indie Mac app.

Open the DMG, drag LidFold to Applications

Then open LidFold from your Applications folder.

Tell macOS it's okay

macOS says it can't verify LidFold. Click Done, then go to System Settings > Privacy & Security, scroll down and click Open Anyway. On macOS 13 or 14, right-click the app and choose Open instead.

Allow Screen Recording

LidFold draws the fold from what's on your screen, so macOS asks for Screen Recording. Turn it on for LidFold, then reopen the app. Nothing is recorded or saved.

Which Macs

Live fold needs the lid angle sensor

Apple quietly added a hinge sensor to recent MacBooks. On those, the fold tracks your hand. Older ones only know open or closed, so LidFold plays a short unfold each time you open the lid.

  • MacBook Pro 14" and 16" from 2021 onward (M1 Pro/Max, M2, M3, M4)
  • MacBook Air M2, M3, M4
  • MacBook Pro 16" (2019), the one Intel model with the sensor
  • M1 MacBook Air, 13" M1/M2 MacBook Pro, other Intel MacBooks: scripted unfold on open
Privacy

Nothing leaves your Mac

The fold is drawn from a live view of your screen, held on the graphics chip only while the lid is moving and thrown away when it settles. There is no code in LidFold that writes to disk or talks to the internet. The source is public, so you can check.
